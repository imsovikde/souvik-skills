## 1. Banking and RBI Regulatory Framework

### A. Reserve Bank of India - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 (RB-IOS 2021)
- **Notification**: RBI Notification CEPD. PR. No. S802/13.01.001/2021-2022 dated November 12, 2021.
- **Authority**: Issued under Section 35A Banking Regulation Act 1949, Section 45L Reserve Bank of India Act 1934, Section 18 Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007.
- **Clause 10 - Grounds of Complaint**: Any deficiency in service provided by a Regulated Entity (bank, NBFC, payment system participant).
- **Clause 11 - Grounds for Non-Maintainability**:
  - **Clause 11(1)**: Grievance must first be represented to the Regulated Entity. The complaint is maintainable only if:
    1. The complaint was rejected wholly or partly by the Regulated Entity; or
    2. The complainant had not received any reply within 30 days of filing the representation; or
    3. The complainant is not satisfied with the reply given by the Regulated Entity.
  - **Clause 11(2) - Limitation Period**: The complaint must be filed:
    1. Within one year after the complainant has received the reply from the Regulated Entity; or
    2. Where no reply is received, within one year and 30 days from the date of the representation.
  - **Clause 11(3) - Statutory Exclusions**: The Ombudsman shall reject any complaint:
    - (a) In respect of commercial transactions between banks or between banks and business counterparties;
    - (b) Pending before or already decided by any court, tribunal, arbitrator, or other forum;
    - (c) Previously settled or disposed of by the Ombudsman.
- **Clause 15(1) & 15(3) - Award and Compensation Ceiling**:
  - The Ombudsman may award compensation for actual consequential loss suffered by the complainant, not exceeding Rs 30,00,000 (Rupees Thirty Lakh).
  - The Ombudsman may award compensation up to Rs 1,00,000 (Rupees One Lakh) for loss of time, expenses incurred, harassment, and mental anguish.
  - **Operational Invariant**: These figures represent maximum discretionary award ceilings. Asserting that a complainant has an automatic statutory entitlement to Rs 30 lakh is an error of law.
- **Clause 17 - Appeal**: Lies to the Appellate Authority (Executive Director in-charge of CEPD, RBI) within 30 days of receipt of the Award.

### B. Customer Protection - Limiting Liability in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions
- **Instrument**: RBI Circular DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18 dated July 6, 2017.
- **Authority**: Reserve Bank of India, Department of Banking Regulation.
- **Paragraph 6 - Zero Liability of a Customer**:
  - **6(a)**: Contributory fraud, negligence, or deficiency on the part of the bank (irrespective of whether the transaction is reported by the customer).
  - **6(b)**: Third-party breach where the deficiency lies neither with the bank nor with the customer but lies elsewhere in the system, provided the customer notifies the bank within three working days of receiving communication regarding the unauthorised transaction.
- **Paragraph 7 - Limited Liability of a Customer**:
  - Customer negligence (e.g. sharing credentials): Customer bears entire loss until the unauthorised transaction is reported. Loss occurring after reporting is borne by the bank.
  - Third-party breach reported between four and seven working days: Customer liability is capped per transaction at:
    - Basic Savings Bank Deposit (BSBD) Accounts: Rs 5,000.
    - Other Savings Accounts, Pre-paid instruments, Current/Cash Credit accounts with limits up to Rs 5 Lakh: Rs 10,000.
    - Credit Cards with credit limits exceeding Rs 5 Lakh: Rs 25,000.
  - Beyond seven working days: Determined strictly as per the bank's Board-approved policy.
- **Paragraph 9 - Reversal Timeline (Shadow Credit)**:
  - The bank must credit the amount involved in the unauthorised transaction to the customer's account within 10 working days from the date of customer notification.
- **Paragraph 10 - Resolution Timeline**: Complaint must be resolved within 90 days.

### C. Harmonisation of Turn Around Time (TAT) and Customer Compensation for Failed Transactions
- **Instrument**: RBI Circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 dated September 20, 2019.
- **Framework Matrix**:
  - **ATM Cash Withdrawal Failed**: Turn Around Time is T + 5 calendar days. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 5 days.
  - **Card to Card Transfer**: TAT is T + 1 day. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 1 day.
  - **Point of Sale (POS) / E-Commerce**: TAT is T + 5 days. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 5 days.
  - **UPI Transactions**: TAT is T + 1 day. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 1 day.
  - **IMPS Transactions**: TAT is T + 1 day. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 1 day.
  - **NACH Mandate Debits**: TAT is T + 1 day. Compensation of Rs 100 per day beyond T + 1 day.

## 2. Consumer Protection Framework

### A. Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act No. 35 of 2019)
- **Commencement**: July 20, 2020 (vide S.O. 2351(E) dated July 15, 2020). Replaced Consumer Protection Act, 1986.
- **Section 2(7) - Consumer Definition**:
  - Excludes any person who obtains goods or avails services for any "commercial purpose".
  - Exception: Commercial purpose does not include use by a person of goods bought and used by him exclusively for the purposes of earning his livelihood by means of self-employment.
  - **Operational Invariant**: Classifying a business B2B dispute or commercial trading account as a consumer dispute without satisfying the self-employment exception violates Section 2(7).
- **Section 2(11) - Deficiency**: Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature, and manner of performance required by law or contract.
- **Section 2(47) - Unfair Trade Practice**: Deceptive practices, misleading representations, non-issuance of bills, or refusing return of defective goods.

### B. Pecuniary Jurisdiction (2021 Rules Revision)
- **Instrument**: Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021 (G.S.R. 892(E) dated December 30, 2021).
- **Current Pecuniary Ceilings**:
  - **District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (Section 34)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed Rs 50,00,000 (Rupees Fifty Lakh).
  - **State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (Section 47)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds Rs 50,00,000 but does not exceed Rs 2,00,00,000 (Rupees Two Crore).
  - **National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (Section 58)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds Rs 2,00,00,000 (Rupees Two Crore).
- **Critical Statutory Metric**: The test under the 2019 Act is the amount "paid as consideration", NOT the aggregate claim amount including compensation (which was the repealed 1986 Act rule).

### C. Limitation and Appeals
- **Section 69(1) - Limitation Period**: A complaint must be filed within 2 years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen. Section 69(2) allows condonation of delay upon showing sufficient cause.
- **Section 41 - Appeal to State Commission**: Within 45 days from order date, subject to 50% pre-deposit of the awarded amount.
- **Section 51 - Appeal to National Commission**: Within 30 days from State Commission order date, subject to 50% pre-deposit.

## 3. Criminal Law Codes (Temporal Demarcation: July 1, 2024)

### A. Temporal Application Invariant
- **Substantive Offence**: Governed by the law in force on the date of commission.
  - Offences committed on or before June 30, 2024: Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC).
  - Offences committed on or after July 1, 2024: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS).
- **Procedure**: Governed by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) for investigations, inquiries, and proceedings initiated on or after July 1, 2024.
- **Section 531 BNSS (Repeal and Savings)**: Pending appeals, applications, trials, inquiries, or investigations initiated prior to July 1, 2024 continue under the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.

### B. Core Statutory Mapping: Substantive Offences
| Subject Matter | IPC 1860 Provision | BNS 2023 Provision | Key Statutory Differences |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cheating** | Section 415, 417, 420 | **Section 318** | Section 318(4) governs cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property (up to 7 years imprisonment and fine). |
| **Criminal Breach of Trust** | Section 405, 406, 409 | **Section 316** | Section 316(2) simple breach; Section 316(5) by clerk/servant; Section 316(7) by banker, merchant, or agent (up to 10 years or life). |
| **Forgery** | Section 463, 465, 468, 471 | **Section 336, 338, 340** | Section 336 defines forgery; Section 338 governs valuable security; Section 340 governs using forged document as genuine. |
| **Criminal Conspiracy** | Section 120A, 120B | **Section 61** | Section 61(2) sets punishments for conspiracy. |
| **Defamation** | Section 499, 500 | **Section 356** | Section 356 retains exceptions and introduces community service as an alternative punishment. |
| **Extortion** | Section 383, 384 | **Section 308** | Section 308 governs extortion definitions and penalties. |

### C. Core Statutory Mapping: Procedural Mechanisms
| Procedural Step | CrPC 1973 Provision | BNSS 2023 Provision | Crucial Procedural Requirements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FIR Registration** | Section 154 | **Section 173** | Section 173(1): Electronic FIR permitted, must be signed within 3 days. Section 173(3): Mandatory preliminary enquiry within 14 days for offences punishable between 3 and 7 years. Section 173(4): Remedy before SP on refusal. |
| **Magisterial Investigation Order** | Section 156(3) | **Section 175(3)** | Application requires prior exhaustion of Section 173(4) before SP, supported by an affidavit detailing compliance. |
| **Private Complaint** | Section 200 | **Section 223** | **Major Shift**: Section 223(1) proviso mandates that no cognizance shall be taken without giving the accused an opportunity of being heard. |
| **Regular Bail** | Section 437, 439 | **Section 480, 483** | Section 480 covers Magistrate bail; Section 483 covers High Court / Sessions Court bail. |
| **Anticipatory Bail** | Section 438 | **Section 482** | Direction for grant of bail to persons apprehending arrest. |
| **Inherent High Court Powers** | Section 482 | **Section 528** | Preserves inherent powers of High Courts to prevent abuse of process or secure ends of justice. |

### D. Electronic Records and Admissibility
- **Statutory Provision**: Section 63 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA). Replaced Section 65B Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
- **Mandatory Certificate**: Section 63(4) BSA mandates a certificate identifying the electronic record and describing the manner of production.
- **Statutory Format**: Must conform to the Schedule to the BSA, consisting of Part A (signed by producing party) and Part B (signed by person in charge of computer/device management).
- **Operational Invariant**: Submitting electronic correspondence, SMS alerts, or server logs without declaring the Section 63 BSA certificate requirement is an evidentiary defect.

## 4. Commercial, Debt Recovery and Insolvency Statutes

### A. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (NI Act)
- **Section 138 - Dishonour of Cheque**:
  - Proviso (a): Cheque presented within 3 months of its date.
  - Proviso (b): Demand notice in writing issued within 30 days of receipt of dishonour memo.
  - Proviso (c): Drawer fails to make payment within 15 days of receipt of notice.
- **Section 142(1)(b) - Limitation**: Complaint must be filed within 1 month from the date the cause of action arises (day 16 to day 45 following notice receipt).
- **Section 143A - Interim Compensation**: Trial court may order drawer to pay interim compensation not exceeding 20% of the cheque amount.
  - **Operational Invariant**: Section 143A is directory and discretionary (*P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers ISPAT Pvt. Ltd.* (2021) 6 SCC 258). It cannot be asserted as an absolute customer right.
- **Section 148 - Appellate Deposit**: Appellate court may direct deposit of a minimum of 20% of the fine or compensation awarded.

### B. SARFAESI Act, 2002
- **Section 13(2)**: 60-day demand notice issued by secured creditor upon classification of account as NPA.
- **Section 13(3A)**: Borrower may submit objections within 60 days. Secured creditor must consider and communicate reasons for rejection within 15 days.
- **Section 13(4)**: Measures taken by secured creditor (possession of secured assets).
- **Section 17(1)**: Application before Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT) within 45 days from the date on which measures under Section 13(4) were taken.
- **Section 18**: Appeal to DRAT with mandatory 50% pre-deposit of the debt (waivable down to 25% by DRAT).

### C. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)
- **Section 4 - Default Threshold**: Minimum threshold of default is Rs 1,00,00,000 (Rupees One Crore), notified vide S.O. 1205(E) dated March 24, 2020.
- **Section 7**: Financial Creditor application before NCLT.
- **Section 8 & 9**: Operational Creditor mandatory 10-day demand notice under Section 8; Section 9 application maintainable only if no notice of existing dispute is received within 10 days.

## 5. Insurance, Securities and Competition Regulators

### A. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)
- **Regulations**: IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests and Allied Matters of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 (notified March 20, 2024).
- **Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017 (as amended 2021)**:
  - **Rule 14(3)**: Grievance must first be represented to the Insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO). Complaint to Ombudsman lies only if:
    - Insurer rejected the representation; or
    - Insurer failed to respond within 30 days; or
    - Complainant is dissatisfied with the response.
  - **Rule 14(3)(b) - Limitation**: Within 1 year from the date of receipt of the rejection order.
  - **Rule 17(1) - Award Limit**: Maximum compensation award of Rs 30,00,000 (Rupees Thirty Lakh).

### B. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
- **SCORES 2.0**: SEBI Circular SEBI/HO/OIAE/OIAE_IAD-1/P/CIR/2024/20 dated March 20, 2024.
  - First Tier: Designated entity must resolve complaint within 21 calendar days.
  - Second Tier: Designated Body review within 10 calendar days upon investor escalation.
- **Section 15T SEBI Act, 1992**: Appeal to Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) within 45 days.

### C. Competition Commission of India (CCI)
- **Statute**: Competition Act, 2002 (as amended by Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023).
- **Section 3**: Prohibition of anti-competitive agreements (cartels, bid-rigging).
- **Section 4**: Prohibition of abuse of dominant position (unfair pricing, limiting production, denial of market access).
- **Section 19**: Information filed before CCI by any person, consumer, or trade association.
- **Section 53B**: Appeal to National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) within 60 days.

## 6. Constitutional Remedies and Precedential Invariants

### A. Article 226 Writs Against Private Bodies (The *Federal Bank* Rule)
- **Binding Precedent**: *Federal Bank Ltd. v. Sagar Thomas & Ors.* (2003) 10 SCC 733; reaffirmed in *St. Mary's Education Society v. Rajendra Prasad Bhargava* (2023) 4 SCC 498.
- **Rule**: A writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India is NOT maintainable against a private bank, private financial institution, or private company for ordinary commercial or contractual disputes.
- **Exception**: Maintainable only if the private entity is discharging a public duty or statutory public function positive in nature.

### B. Exhaustion of Alternative Remedies (The *Whirlpool* Doctrine)
- **Binding Precedents**: *Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks, Mumbai* (1998) 8 SCC 1; *Harbanslal Sahnia v. Indian Oil Corpn. Ltd.* (2003) 2 SCC 107.
- **Rule**: High Courts will refuse to exercise writ jurisdiction under Article 226 if an efficacious alternative statutory remedy (e.g. DRT under SARFAESI, NCLAT under IBC, Ombudsman, or Consumer Commission) is available.
- **Four Strict Exceptions**:
  1. The writ petition is filed for the enforcement of any of the Fundamental Rights;
  2. There has been a violation of the principles of natural justice;
  3. The order or proceedings are wholly without jurisdiction;
  4. The vires of an Act or statutory rule is challenged.

### C. Supervisory Jurisdiction over Civil Courts
- **Binding Precedent**: *Radhey Shyam & Anr. v. Chhabi Nath & Ors.* (2015) 5 SCC 423.
- **Rule**: Judicial orders of civil courts are not amenable to writ of certiorari under Article 226. Remedy lies exclusively under Article 227 (supervisory jurisdiction of High Courts).

### D. Mandatory Statutory Pre-Suit Notices
- **Section 80 Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC)**: Mandatory 2-month prior notice in writing before instituting any suit against the Government or a public officer in respect of an official act.
