# Maintainability, Limitation, and Jurisdiction Audit Manual

This manual provides decision rules, statutory thresholds, and limitation schedules to determine whether a contemplated legal filing or regulatory complaint under Indian law is maintainable.

---

## 1. Multi-Tiered Internal Grievance Redressal Architecture

Before filing any statutory regulatory complaint, internal grievance remedies must be exhausted according to the prescribed tiers.

```text
Tier 1: Frontline Branch / Grievance Officer (Branch Manager / Customer Support)
   | (Unresolved or 30 days lapse)
   v
Tier 2: Principal Nodal Officer (PNO) / Internal Ombudsman of Institution
   | (Rejection / Unsatisfactory resolution / 30 days total lapse)
   v
Tier 3: Statutory Ombudsman / Regulatory Forum (RBI Ombudsman, IRDAI Bima Bharosa, SEBI SCORES)
   | (Disputed complex facts or exhaustion of ombudsman)
   v
Tier 4: Judicial / Quasi-Judicial Forum (Consumer Commission, DRT, Commercial Court, High Court)
```

### Banking Redressal (RBI Mandate)
- **Step 1**: Lodge formal grievance with the Bank's Branch Manager or online grievance portal. Retain ticket reference number and automated confirmation timestamp.
- **Step 2**: If unresolved within 10 to 15 days, escalate to the Bank's Principal Nodal Officer (PNO).
- **Step 3**: If no response is received within 30 days from the initial representation, or if the reply is unsatisfactory, the matter is ripe for filing before the RBI Ombudsman under Clause 11(1) of the RB-IOS 2021.
- **Maintainability Rule**: An RBI Ombudsman complaint filed on Day 15 without a rejection letter will be rejected under Clause 11(1) for premature filing.

### Insurance Redressal (IRDAI Mandate)
- **Step 1**: Submit formal representation to the Insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO).
- **Step 2**: Retain proof of delivery or email acknowledgement.
- **Step 3**: If unresolved or rejected within 30 days, file before the Insurance Ombudsman under Rule 14(3) of the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017.

### Securities Redressal (SEBI SCORES 2.0)
- **Step 1**: Register complaint on SCORES 2.0 portal against listed entity or market intermediary.
- **Step 2**: The entity is allotted 21 calendar days to resolve the grievance.
- **Step 3**: If dissatisfied, investor may trigger Designated Body review within 15 calendar days; the Designated Body has 10 calendar days to review.

## 2. Pecuniary Jurisdiction Matrix

Jurisdictional value must be calculated using the specific metric prescribed by the governing statute.

| Forum | Governing Statutory Provision | Prescribed Metric | Pecuniary Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **District Consumer Commission** | Section 34 CPA 2019 read with 2021 Rules | Value of goods or services **paid as consideration** | Up to Rs 50,00,000 (Fifty Lakh) |
| **State Consumer Commission** | Section 47 CPA 2019 read with 2021 Rules | Value of goods or services **paid as consideration** | Above Rs 50,00,000 up to Rs 2,00,00,000 (Two Crore) |
| **National Consumer Commission (NCDRC)** | Section 58 CPA 2019 read with 2021 Rules | Value of goods or services **paid as consideration** | Exceeding Rs 2,00,00,000 (Two Crore) |
| **Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT)** | Section 1(4) Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 (RDBA) | Debt due to bank or financial institution | Minimum Rs 20,00,000 (Twenty Lakh) |
| **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)** | Section 4 Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 | Minimum amount of default | Minimum Rs 1,00,00,000 (One Crore) |
| **Commercial Court (District Level)** | Section 3 Commercial Courts Act, 2015 | Specified Value of commercial dispute | Minimum Rs 3,00,000 (Three Lakh) |
| **Civil Judge (Junior Division)** | State Civil Courts Acts (e.g. Maharashtra, Delhi, Karnataka) | Valuation of suit for court fee and jurisdiction | Varies by State (typically up to Rs 5 Lakh) |

### Key Pecuniary Trap: Consumer Consideration vs Compensation
Under the repealed Consumer Protection Act 1986, jurisdiction was determined by the total value of goods plus compensation claimed. Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, the sole criterion is the value paid as consideration. Pleading Rs 10 lakh consideration plus Rs 95 lakh mental agony does not elevate jurisdiction to the State Commission; it remains squarely before the District Commission.

## 3. Territorial Jurisdiction Matrix

A complaint or plaint must demonstrate an authentic jurisdictional nexus within the territorial limits of the chosen forum.

### Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Section 34(2))
A consumer complaint may be instituted before the District Commission within whose local limits:
1. The opposite party resides, carries on business, or has a branch office; or
2. Any of the opposite parties resides or carries on business (with permission of District Commission); or
3. The cause of action, wholly or in part, arises; or
4. **The complainant resides or personally works for gain**.
*Note*: Criterion 4 represents a major reform introduced by the 2019 Act, enabling a consumer to file in their own home district.

### Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (Section 20)
Suits must be instituted where the defendant resides, carries on business, or where the cause of action arises, wholly or in part.

### Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (Section 142(2))
- If the cheque is delivered for collection through an account: Jurisdiction lies where the branch of the bank where the payee or holder in due course maintains the account is situated.
- If the cheque is presented for payment otherwise through an account: Jurisdiction lies where the drawer maintains the account.

## 4. Limitation Periods Master Schedule

A claim filed one day beyond the statutory limitation period is liable to summary dismissal unless specific condonation provisions apply.

| Action / Forum | Governing Statute | Trigger Event | Limitation Period | Condonation Provision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Consumer Complaint** | Section 69 CPA 2019 | Date on which cause of action arose | **2 Years** | Section 69(2): Condonation upon sufficient cause shown. |
| **RBI Integrated Ombudsman** | Clause 11(2) RB-IOS 2021 | Receipt of reply from bank, or date of representation | **1 Year** (from reply) or **1 Year + 30 Days** (if no reply) | No general condonation power. |
| **Insurance Ombudsman** | Rule 14(3)(b) Insurance Ombudsman Rules | Receipt of rejection order from insurer | **1 Year** | No condonation beyond 1 year. |
| **Cheque Dishonour Notice** | Section 138 Proviso (b) NI Act | Receipt of memo of dishonour from bank | **30 Days** | Strict compliance mandatory. |
| **Cheque Dishonour Complaint** | Section 142(1)(b) NI Act | Expiry of 15-day notice cure period | **1 Month** | Section 142(1)(b) Proviso: Condonation upon sufficient cause. |
| **SARFAESI DRT Application** | Section 17(1) SARFAESI Act | Date on which Section 13(4) measures taken | **45 Days** | Strict limitation; Section 5 Limitation Act applicability disputed. |
| **Appeal to State Commission** | Section 41 CPA 2019 | Date of receipt of District Commission order | **45 Days** | Proviso: Condonation upon sufficient cause. |
| **Appeal to NCDRC** | Section 51 CPA 2019 | Date of receipt of State Commission order | **30 Days** | Proviso: Condonation upon sufficient cause. |
| **Appeal to SAT** | Section 15T SEBI Act | Date of receipt of SEBI / Stock Exchange order | **45 Days** | Section 15T(3): Condonation upon sufficient cause. |
| **Appeal to NCLAT** | Section 61(2) IBC 2016 | Date of receipt of NCLT order | **30 Days** | Proviso: Condonation up to 15 days maximum; absolute bar beyond 45 days. |
| **Suit on Contract / Debt** | Article 14/55 Limitation Act 1963 | Date of breach or due date of payment | **3 Years** | Section 5 Limitation Act does NOT apply to original suits. |

## 5. Writ Maintainability Decision Tree (Article 226)

Before advising or drafting a writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, follow this decision tree:

```text
Is the Respondent an Article 12 State or Public Authority?
   |
   +---> YES: Is an efficacious alternative statutory remedy available?
   |           |
   |           +---> YES: Does the case fall under one of the 4 Whirlpool Exceptions?
   |           |           |
   |           |           +---> YES: Writ Maintainable.
   |           |           +---> NO:  Writ Barred. Direct user to statutory forum.
   |           |
   |           +---> NO:  Writ Maintainable under Article 226.
   |
   +---> NO (Private Entity): Is the Private Entity discharging a statutory public duty?
               |
               +---> YES: Does the challenged action relate directly to that public duty?
               |           |
               |           +---> YES: Writ Maintainable (*St. Mary's* exception).
               |           +---> NO:  Writ Barred under *Federal Bank* rule.
               |
               +---> NO:  Writ Strictly Non-Maintainable (*Federal Bank* rule).
```

### The Four *Whirlpool* Exceptions
High Courts will bypass the alternative remedy rule only when:
1. Fundamental Rights under Part III of the Constitution are infringed.
2. The principles of natural justice have been violated (e.g. ex-parte order without notice).
3. The impugned order or proceeding is wholly without jurisdiction.
4. The constitutional validity (vires) of an Act or subordinate rule is challenged.

## 6. Pre-Suit Notice Audit

Verify mandatory statutory notice compliance:
- **Section 80 Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**: Mandatory 2-month written notice before filing a suit against the Government or a public officer in their official capacity. Pleading urgent relief without notice requires an explicit application under Section 80(2).
- **Section 138 Proviso (b) NI Act**: Mandatory 30-day notice demanding payment of the cheque amount.
- **Section 8 IBC 2016**: Mandatory 10-day demand notice for operational creditors prior to filing under Section 9.
